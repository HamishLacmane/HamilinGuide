About Me

Born and raised in NW London, UK, and not exactly born with a silver spoon in my mouth. So yes, I care about whether food is actually worth the money.

Chicken Cottage and Wembley Market were considered a treat growing up. I can afford to spend more on food these days, but just because I can doesn't mean I should. If it isn't worth the money, I'll tell you.

So, what can I actually eat?

As a Hindu, there are some restrictions on what ends up on my plate. Then there are the restrictions that are entirely my own doing.

Growing up Indian, I'm used to having a lot of flavour and plenty of spices in my food. So bland food isn't really my thing. If I'm paying for a meal, I want it to taste good — preferably without spending the rest of the evening regretting it.

I'm a picky eater. Or, as I prefer to call it, a man with an exquisite palate.

The quick version:

✅ Can eat

  1. Vegan & vegetarian food
  2. Cod — historically only in fish & chips, but these days I'll occasionally branch out into something a little more boujie, like miso black cod
  3. Prawns — only when Mum makes them

❌ Can't eat

  • Beef
  • Pork
  • Lamb / mutton — Dad's keema is probably one of the best dishes I've eaten, but unfortunately my stomach doesn't agree with it, so these days it's a no-go
  • Cheeses made with animal rennet
  • Mushrooms
  • Most food on the bone
  • Most egg-based dishes

⚠️ Depends on the day

  • No onion, no garlic on certain religious days
  • Chicken wings — very occasionally

Most of the time, you'll find me eating vegetarian food, Nando's, fried chicken burgers or gelato. But every now and again, I'll happily go a little boujie. Give me a good miso black cod and suddenly I'm pretending I understand fine dining.
